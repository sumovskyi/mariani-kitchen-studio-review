Kitchen Cost & Scope · Toronto

Understand what the money is changing before you compare the totals.

Two kitchen proposals can show different prices because they include different rooms, cabinetry, interiors, materials, appliances, installation, site work, responsibilities, and unknowns.

Review cost and scope

The useful financial question is not whether every upgrade sounds premium. It is which decisions materially improve the room, what each proposal includes, who owns the connections, and where uncertainty can become an expensive change later.

Keep the room connectedLayout, appliances, services, surfaces, lighting and cabinetry must be priced against the same plan.

Spend where use changesStorage, durable touchpoints, working light, appliance integration and precise fit deserve priority.

A useful comparison begins only after the included work, exclusions, responsibilities and still-unknown decisions are visible. Maryani can review those differences against the room you actually want to build.

01

Start with the same scope.

Compare the room, cabinetry quantity, interiors, appliances, surfaces, lighting, demolition, services, installation, finishing, and connected renovation—not only the cabinet line.

02

Separate consequential choices.

Layout, storage, durable touchpoints, working light, appliance integration, and precise fit can change daily use. More products do not automatically make the room better.

03

Make responsibility visible.

Clarify who measures, designs, coordinates appliances and services, prepares the site, installs, adjusts, protects the home, and resolves the work around the cabinetry.
